# Common movie formats: which stream layouts play filtered exactly

**2026-11-24 · lab research and plugin change, desktop mpv only · branch `feat/common-formats`.** Owner decision (question dialog, 2026-11-24): *"Your real movies can now be marked and approved, but only the lab clip's format plays filtered. What should I do next?"* → *"Measure common movie formats (Recommended) — Make lab copies in the most common formats (MKV with H.264 or HEVC video, and AAC, AC3 or EAC3 sound) and prove filtered playback is exact on each, so typical movies can play filtered."*

## Answer

**5 of 12 common layouts now play filtered exactly** (desktop mpv, decoded PCM, through the plugin on both Jellyfin 10.11.11 and 12.4.0, same result on both). They are the ones with Dolby sound: AC-3 5.1 and E-AC-3 5.1 in MKV (H.264 or HEVC Main 10) and E-AC-3 5.1 in MP4. They need the static player setting `cons_noisegen=1`. Every layout with AAC, FLAC or Opus sound, and the file with a default subtitle track, is refused, each for a measured reason. Getting there needed three real fixes: MKV cut lists were written on the wrong clock, Matroska frame boundaries tie, and MP4 E-AC-3 files could not be bound at all.

**Demoted 2026-11-25 (branch `feat/boundary-sweep`):** every admitted common-format and subtitle row was removed from the register, because each was measured at one cut position only. A layout is admitted again only after it passes the cut-position sweep on each server line.

**Update 2026-11-25:** these results hold at the one measured cut position. At other positions mpv's truncating sample rounding can put one sample wrong at a join, including the last marked sample (measured on MP4 E-AC-3; see [AAC](#aac-is-there-a-truly-exact-path-owner-decision-r20-2026-11-25)). AAC has no exact path yet.

"Exact" is the same bar as the [audio-at-the-join research](../exact-audio-2026-11-22/01-audio-at-the-join.md): with picture and sound from one playback, every kept frame appears once and in order, no marked frame appears, and every kept sample on every channel equals mpv's uninterrupted decode of the same file with exactly the marked samples removed. No alignment, no tolerance. This must also hold for fresh starts before, at and after the join and for seeks across and onto it. Anything less is stated, and that layout gets no offer.

## What was wrong, and what changed

| Finding | Effect before | Change |
|---|---|---|
| mpv reads cut-list times as the **file's own timestamps**. The plan's source zero is the earliest stream start, and in Matroska that is before file time zero (encoder priming: AAC −21 ms, AC-3/E-AC-3 −5 ms, Opus −7 ms). | Every MKV cut landed late by that offset: one marked frame shown, one kept frame lost, sound misplaced. MP4 hid this because it starts at 0. | The renderer (adapter revision 2) adds the file time of source zero, derived exactly from the verified track clocks. If the tracks disagree, or zero is not a whole microsecond, it refuses (`UnverifiedPlayerClock`). |
| In Matroska, a boundary **exactly on a frame instant** keeps that frame (mpv reads Matroska frame times a hair early). | One marked frame at the join; a seek to just before the join landed on a marked frame. | For Matroska with AC-3/E-AC-3, interior boundaries are written 5 µs (a quarter sample) before the instant. Measured: the same kept frames and the same kept samples. The same shift *loses* a sample with AAC and FLAC, so it is not used there. |
| A shared boundary cannot be both "just before" for picture and "just after" for sound. | — | The **split-partition** form (picture and sound in separate partitions of the same source) is implemented and measured. FLAC in MKV is exact on the straight cut and seeks with it. AAC and AC-3 lose their decoder history in a sound-only partition, so they keep the joint form. FLAC is still refused, for a different reason (below). |
| AC-3/E-AC-3 decoders add dither from a random state. | Kept sound differed by 1 LSB after the join. | Measured with the static player setting `ad-lavc-o=cons_noisegen=1` (named in the evidence tuple's device mode). A player without it is a different tuple. |
| The plugin hard-coded the lab clip's layout (1280×720, 25 fps, stereo AAC-LC, MP4, track indexes 0/1). | Every other file: `EvidenceTupleUnknown` before the register was even read. | `EvidenceSourceLabel` derives the layout from the verified file. It records container family, every track's codec, profile, bit depth, range, geometry, rate, channels, time base and clock anchor, which sound track plays, and the subtitles. The register then decides. A layout the label cannot state (no or ambiguous default sound, cover art or attachments, unknown facts) is refused. PlaybackInfo, the profile check and the projected source follow the measured layout: container family, codecs and the default sound track index. |

## Refused, and why

- **MP4/MKV + AAC.** AAC with perceptual noise substitution (PNS, which ffmpeg's encoder uses by default) fills noise bands from a decoder random generator, and after a join its state differs: up to 3 LSB on one channel, never marked material. The same file encoded without PNS measured exact on the straight cut. In Matroska, millisecond timestamps cannot place AAC's 21.333 ms frames: the plain and cut-list paths disagree by 15–17 samples. The [hard fixture's AAC](../exact-audio-2026-11-22/01-audio-at-the-join.md) (no PNS, identical channels) stays exact as before.
- **MKV + FLAC.** The straight cut and seeks are exact with split partitions, but a fresh start *exactly at the join* emits one sample from before the start position, and that sample is the last marked one (source sample 504959). One marked sample is a leak.
- **MKV + Opus.** 48 samples before the join differ (Opus pre-roll/overlap).
- **Subtitles.** A subtitle line that lies wholly inside a mark never appears. A line that *starts inside* a mark and ends after it is shown after the join with its full text. The picture and sound are exact, but the line's words may belong to the marked content. That is a policy question, not a timing fault (see the owner decision in the report).

## What this proves and what it does not

- **Proves:** desktop mpv 0.40 in the lab container, decoding to PCM, plays these synthetic layouts exactly through the plugin's real PlaybackInfo, ticket and cut-list path on Jellyfin 10.11.11 and 12.4.0. This covers one cut position per layout and the measured starts and seeks.
- **Does not prove:** any TV app (Wholphin or otherwise), Android's audio path, HDMI output, a real movie's encoder settings, other cut positions, persisted resume or restart. **No TV or Wholphin tuple is promoted from this evidence.** The rows are `experimental` (lab only), for the exact tuple `mpv 0.40.0` + image digest + device mode + plugin build + Jellyfin version.
- **Passthrough.** AC-3/E-AC-3 bitstreamed undecoded to a receiver cannot be cut to the sample by any player; at best whole coded frames (32 ms) are dropped and the receiver decodes the rest. The design therefore treats passthrough as a **different output mode that is refused**. Evidence rows exist only for decoded-PCM device modes, a passthrough mode never matches them (contract test `DecodedPcmEvidenceNeverAdmitsPassthroughOrAnotherDevice`), and a TV measurement must verify decoded PCM arrived (the HDMI harness already checks for bitstream on every take). A future frame-granular passthrough adapter would need its own action semantics, not a relabelled cut.

## AAC: is there a truly exact path? (owner decision R20, 2026-11-25)

**No exact AAC path yet.** Owner decision (question dialog, 2026-11-25): *"AAC sound (the most common stereo format) is off by tiny, inaudible amounts after a cut. No marked sound plays. Should that count as exact?"* → *"Keep it off, measure real AAC (Recommended)."* Every AAC layout stays refused. Three separate things stand in the way, and only the first depends on how the file was encoded:

1. **Noise substitution (PNS) has no fixed sample values.** FFmpeg's AAC decoder fills noise bands from one pseudo-random generator, `ac->random_state`. It is seeded once when the decoder opens (`0x1f2e3d4c`), it is never reset by a flush or seek, and no decoder option changes it (FFmpeg 7.1 and 8.0 `libavcodec/aac/aacdec.c`, `aacdec_proc_template.c`; the lab mpv decodes with FFmpeg 7.1.5 whichever Jellyfin line serves the file). After any join or seek the noise therefore differs from an uninterrupted decode, and the same is true of the unfiltered original after a seek. Restoring the state would mean decoding the marked sound, which a cut list cannot express. The AAC standard defines these bands as random, so no decoder has "the right" values. Under R20 a file that uses PNS can never be exact. HE-AAC adds a second history-dependent generator (the SBR noise and sine indexes in `aacsbr.c`), so it falls under the same verdict. The pinned image's FDK has no SBR, so HE-AAC was not measured.
2. **mpv rounds sound boundaries by truncation.** `mp_aframe_clip_timestamps` (mpv 0.40, `audio/aframe.c`) converts `(boundary − frame time) × 48000` to an integer by truncation. Plan boundaries are whole milliseconds, so they always sit exactly on a sample, and floating-point noise decides whether the result is one sample short. With PNS-free AAC in MP4 and two-segment joint cut lists played directly in mpv (`format-matrix.py` measures one position), 5 of 12 other cut positions came out one sample wrong at the join, and some of those played the **last marked sample**. The same happens to the already-measured MP4 E-AC-3 layout: 3 of 7 positions wrong, one of them a marked sample (output sample 312959 equal to source sample 554879). The cut list cannot fix this for cuts on picture frames: the picture needs the boundary on or before the frame instant, the sound needs it a fraction of a sample after. Separate sound partitions do fix the rounding (that is how FLAC's straight cut became exact), but in MP4 they cost AAC its decoder history. The sound-only partition starts decoding at the boundary packet (mpv gets no seek pre-roll from MP4; `--hr-seek-demuxer-offset` and `--demuxer-seekable-cache=no` did not change it), and 448–576 samples after each segment start were wrong. A fresh start also begins one kept sample early where the original at the same frame does not.
3. **Matroska places AAC by rounded milliseconds.** After each seek mpv takes the first decoded block's millisecond time, minus the stored codec delay, and then counts samples (`filters/f_decoder_wrapper.c` keeps an interpolated time within 1 ms of the container's). Block times are rounded to 1 ms while AAC frames are 21.333 ms, so the sound after a join lands up to 16 samples off, depending on which block the seek happened to start on, plus one sample from truncating the codec delay. This is the same without PNS. A renderer correction would have to predict mpv's choice of starting block from the file's cue points; not attempted.

**What would make AAC exact:** a file without PNS, **and** a player that rounds sound boundaries to the nearest sample (an upstream mpv change a person would have to propose), **and**, for Matroska, a sample-accurate audio clock. With PNS-free MP4 AAC the straight cut and every seek were exact at the measured position; only the rounding stands between that and a general claim.

### What real AAC releases use

- **Scene rules (2020):** AAC only as VBR AAC-LC, encoded with Apple's encoder (qaac `--tvbr 82`), FDK (`--bitrate-mode 4`) or Nero (`-q 0.4`). Multichannel HD sound is normally AC-3/E-AC-3 instead ([WEB ruleset v2.0](https://scenerules.org/t.html?id=2020_WDX_unformatted.nfo), [x265 ruleset](https://opentrackers.org/scenerules.org/html/2020_X265.html), [encoding-hun SD rules](https://github.com/encoding-hun/rules-and-standards/blob/master/series-and-movies-x264-sd-and-480p.md)).
- **Home encodes:** HandBrake uses FFmpeg's AAC encoder on Windows and Linux and Apple's CoreAudio encoder on macOS; FDK is not in official builds ([HandBrake audio codecs](https://handbrake.fr/docs/en/latest/technical/audio-codecs.html), [HandBrake 0.10.0 news](https://handbrake.fr/news.php?article=27)). FFmpeg's encoder uses PNS by default (`-aac_pns`, [FFmpeg codecs documentation](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-codecs.html)).
- **FDK:** AAC-LC uses PNS only at 28–48 kbit/s per channel (`libAACenc/src/pnsparam.cpp`, `levelTable_lowComplexity`, [fdk-aac](https://github.com/mstorsjo/fdk-aac)). Scene stereo at VBR mode 4 (about 128 kbit/s) is above that; 5.1 at common rates is inside it.
- **Apple (qaac) and Nero:** not measured; neither runs in the lab. An FFmpeg encoder developer states that no other encoder used PNS, which would put Apple's encoder in the PNS-free group ([Hydrogenaudio thread](https://hydrogenaudio.org/index.php/topic,129691.0.html)). This is unverified, and a PNS-free file still hits the rounding and Matroska problems above.

So typical AAC movies split into a PNS group (FFmpeg-encoded, low-rate or multichannel FDK) and a probably PNS-free group (scene stereo from qaac or FDK). The PNS-free group would become exact in MP4 only with the mpv rounding fix.

### Telling PNS files apart (evaluated, not wired into the plugin)

`aac_noise.py` decodes the sound track twice with FFmpeg's decoder: once alone, and once behind a short prefix that is known to move the noise generator (checked each time by decoding the prefix twice). From the third frame on, the two decodes can differ only through the generator, so any difference means the track draws noise. It found PNS exactly where the encoder settings predict (FFmpeg native, FDK 96k stereo, FDK 5.1) and none in FFmpeg with `-aac_pns 0` or in FDK 192k stereo. Cost: two decodes of the sound track, roughly seconds to tens of seconds per film. A cheaper bitstream scan would have to Huffman-decode every scale factor and spectral section to reach the band types, which amounts to a decoder. It is not worth adding to the plugin while PNS-free AAC is still not exact.
