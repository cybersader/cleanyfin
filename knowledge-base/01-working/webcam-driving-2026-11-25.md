# Driving an onn. 4K Pro with a webcam reference

Wireless navigation works on the real box. **Do not use the current webcam framing as the sole source of focus.** Google TV often shows a preview on the right and the actual highlighted row on the left. For example, the camera read “About” and “Android TV OS version 14” clearly while the active screen was **System** with **About** highlighted. After entering About, the camera sometimes showed a blank pane while the focused row and build number were plainly visible in the ADB image.

## Before sending a key

1. Have the operator frame **both panes** and the Home focus strip, with the adjacent computer display excluded. Verify the framing after closing and reopening the camera; do not assume the first frame establishes a repeatable pose. Stop if safe framing cannot be maintained.
2. Connect only to the explicitly approved, already-paired wireless debugging endpoint. The helper does not discover, connect, pair, or disconnect. Launch Settings directly rather than activating an uncertain Home tile. Settings may **resume its last subpage** instead of opening its root.
3. Run one command per decision. For an offline example, `python3 tools/playback-lab/drive_look.py BACK --dry-run`. For live use, omit `--dry-run`, supply `--target` with the known connected TLS endpoint and `--crop` with the verified TV-only rectangle `X,Y,W,H`. The output identifies a private webcam PNG, an ADB PNG, and the timings; the images are sequential, not simultaneous. Delete the private pair after comparing. If capture fails, assume the key **may already have taken effect**; do not replay it blindly.
4. Wait **450 ms after ADB acknowledges the action** before starting the webcam grab. Zero delay gave a legible Storage heading but doubled, moving text below it. With 450 ms the sampled Settings labels were steady. This is a starting interval, not a guarantee: require a readable, stationary highlight before selecting. If the highlight is missing, use the verified ADB reference or stop for reframing; waiting longer cannot fix a missing pane.
5. Back out, verify Home, disconnect the specific test target, and release the camera. Do not send a gimbal command to prove parking.

## Gimbalatch: owner-approved one-shots

1. **First attended check:** after the owner presses the 5-minute **check** button, run `python3 -I -B tools/playback-lab/drive_look.py --camera gimbalatch --probe` once. It reads the installed local-witness credentials, binds, reads live control ranges/values once, and ends the session. It sends no TV key or PTZ command. Probe may wake the camera; observe it in person. Without a pending grant, stop and wait for the owner. Do not replay a bind after a lost response; ask the owner to revoke and verify first.
2. **Capture later:** `python3 -I -B tools/playback-lab/drive_look.py --camera gimbalatch --capture` requests one frame and reports its byte count, not pixels. A probe-only grant cannot capture. Gimbalatch v1 also requires the approved preset to have been selected **in the same bound session**. Monitor and park are not calibrated yet, and this client never sends aim or writes presets. The capture command is blocked on a later, separately owner-authorized aiming path; a fresh capture grant or manually pointing the camera does not satisfy this requirement. Do not try a live capture or Gimbalatch-backed navigation until that path is ready.
3. With `--camera gimbalatch`, navigation validates the cropped camera PNG and ADB PNG in memory and reports only timing, not a visual pair. It never saves or displays camera pixels. The direct V4L2 backend and synthetic dry run still write their private PNG pairs. A camera refusal after a TV key does not undo that key.

## Reference measurements: 2026-11-25

Across **21 manual navigation actions**, Home → resumed Settings → System → About → Home, the first comparison-ready webcam image arrived **1.74–2.71 s** after action dispatch (median **2.00 s**, including the specified wait). The builds and menus were read on-screen: Android TV OS **14**, build **`URO3.260203.035.A1.15640252`**. The build came from the screencap, not the webcam. No family app or account page was opened, and no setting was changed. Only Home, Back, D-pad movement and the Settings intent were used.

For two Back transitions with no added wait, the first visibly changed frames had V4L2 capture timestamps **1.75 s** and **1.78 s** after key dispatch; their PNGs returned at **1.86 s** and **1.89 s**. Those are **upper bounds** on the display change, not key-to-photon latency: the camera was closed before the grab. The ADB PNG was collected after the webcam PNG, so matching content establishes the navigation result, not the time the TV first drew it.

## Link 2 findings and camera-service requirements

- `/dev/video3` delivered single **MJPEG, 1920×1080** frames. The comparison backend cropped in the capture process, returned PNG and exited on each grab. No audio, second camera node, control queries, or PTZ/tracking commands were used. Capture format/resolution were negotiated; that is not a gimbal control.
- Spaced-out single grabs took **1.22–2.19 s** from starting the capture process to receiving its first PNG. On two immediate, separate reopens, that fell to **0.20–0.21 s**; the two camera timestamps were **253 ms** and **258 ms** apart. This measures single-shot reopen cadence, **not** the camera's continuous-stream FPS.
- Starting capture made frames available; physical wake, motion during open/close and automatic parking were not independently observable. The initial full image and the later safe crop are not comparable evidence of a fixed pose. No attempt was made to counteract possible movement. Capture stopped and the node had no process holder at the end.
- The service should expose a one-shot **TV-only region**, enforce exclusivity/expiry, and report capture time on a monotonic clock separately from request start and image delivery. Include dimensions, freshness/sequence and whether a known crop/pose is still valid; refuse delivery if not. Default to no retention and no audio; expose confirmed release/park state if hardware supports it. A future backend only needs to implement `Camera.grab()` for image delivery; richer timing should come from the service, not guesses based on request duration.
